## Abstract

Three previously validated improvements were combined and reevaluated at stage 2: planning-head refinement (`planifls`), corrected 3D rotation augmentation (`rot3dv2`), and aux-2D stage-1 pretraining (`ptaux2d`). The expected fully additive gain on planning did not materialize. Across the completed cross-host runs, `ptaux2d_ppdeformmm_planifls` is the strongest planning config: DGX job 3614 reached `L2=0.5057` with `obj_box_col=0.043%`, and Narval job 59670984 reached `L2=0.5122` with `obj_box_col=0.055%`. Relative to the public-checkpoint `ppdeformmm_planifls_rot3dv2` repeats (`L2=0.5351/0.5398`, `obj_box_col=0.048%/0.052%` on DGX/Narval), aux2d improves trajectory accuracy consistently and improves collision on DGX, but not on Narval. Both remain substantially ahead of the SparseDrive stage-2 baseline (`L2=0.636`, `obj_box_col=0.133%`).

Adding rot3dv2 on top of aux2d pretraining improved perception metrics but slightly hurt planning. The full combination `ptaux2d_ppdeformmm_planifls_rot3dv2` reached the strongest detection quality in this report (`NDS=0.5528`, `mAP=0.4470`) but regressed to `L2=0.5270` and `obj_box_col=0.062%` relative to aux2d-only. The conclusion is that aux2d and the planning-head refinement combine cleanly for planning, while rot3dv2 is beneficial primarily for detection in this setting and is not yet additive on the planning metrics that matter most.

Two follow-up experiments have since completed. **`ptaux2p5d_ppdeformmm_planifls`** (Narval 59814262, DGX 3636) tests whether the aux2p5d depth branch transfers from stage-1 detection quality to stage-2 planning. The results are cross-host inconsistent: Narval shows a marginal planning improvement over aux2d-only (`L2=0.5101` vs `0.5122`, `NDS=0.5501` vs `0.5479`), while DGX shows the opposite — detection improves substantially (`NDS=0.5550`, `mAP=0.4461`) but planning regresses (`L2=0.5232`, `obj_box_col=0.065%`). This host-dependent pattern suggests the depth branch does not reliably transfer to planning gains. **`ptaux2d_ppdeformmm_planifls_numdemapx2`** (Narval 59814383) doubles the planning context (`num_det=100`, `num_map=20`) but yields results nearly identical to the standard-context baseline (`L2=0.5100` vs `0.5122`, `obj_box_col=0.055%` vs `0.055%`), indicating the planner is not bottlenecked by the number of detection or map instances it conditions on.

A further follow-up has now completed. **`ptauxdnrot_ppdeformmm`** (Killarney 3300814) loads the new combined stage-1 pretrain `sparsedrive_stage1_dn_rot3d_aux2p5d.pth` (from the Apollo run that reached stage-1 `mAP=0.4558`, `NDS=0.5633`, `mAP_normal=0.5817`) on top of `planpredtrajdeformmm` with no `planifls` and no stage-2 rot3d. Detection rises to `NDS=0.5609`, `mAP=0.4576`, `mAP_normal=0.5786` — the strongest perception in the family and tracking the Apollo stage-1 numbers nearly 1:1 — but planning regresses to `L2=0.5612`, `obj_box_col=0.061%`, behind both the matched-architecture `ppdeform` baseline (`L2=0.542`, `obj_box_col=0.047%`) and the planning-first leader `ptaux2d_ppdeformmm_planifls` DGX 3614 (`L2=0.5057`). This continues the rot3dv2 / aux2p5d pattern: richer stage-1 supervision improves perception robustly but does not convert into planning gains.

The matching `planifls` follow-up has now also completed. **`ptauxdnrot_ppdeformmm_planifls`** (Killarney 3305029) reaches `L2=0.5216`, `obj_box_col=0.090%`, `NDS=0.5578`, `mAP=0.4497`, `mAP_normal=0.5806`. Adding `planifls` to the dn+rot3d+aux2p5d pretrain recovers ~0.04 of `L2` over the no-`planifls` variant (`0.5612 → 0.5216`) but still does not match `ptaux2d_ppdeformmm_planifls` DGX 3614 (`L2=0.5057`, `obj_box_col=0.043%`). Detection remains the second-strongest in the family (`mAP_normal=0.5806`, only `0.0020` behind the no-`planifls` ptauxdnrot run). The takeaway is that `planifls` does help on top of the richer pretrain — it is not architecture-specific to aux2d — but the additional stage-1 signals (det DN + rot3d + aux2p5d) do not push planning past the aux2d-only ceiling.

## Introduction

Three independent lines of improvement have each been validated individually against the SparseDrive stage-2 baseline (`sparsedrive_r50_stage2_4gpu_bs24`, L2=0.636, CR=0.133%):

**Planning head refinement** (`plan_refine` batch, 2026-04-02). Multi-stage cumulative planning refinement combined with endpoint deformable attention and ego instance feature injection (`planpredtrajdeformmm_planinstfeat_laststage`) achieves L2=0.522, CR=0.047% — an 18% reduction in L2 and 65% reduction in collision rate. The mechanism is straightforward: attending at predicted far-horizon trajectory endpoints gives the model direct image evidence of what lies along the planned path, rather than at the current ego position. Restricting the ego instance feature injection to the final decoder stage captures temporal tracking context without suppressing the obstacle-aware attention established in earlier stages.

**3D rotation augmentation** (`rot3d` batch, 2026-04-15). Fixing label-frame consistency in `BBoxRotation` (rot3dv2) — rotating map geometry, agent/ego future trajectories, and ego velocity alongside the 3D boxes — makes rotation augmentation effective end-to-end for the first time. Applied at stage-2 with the public pretrain, rot3dv2 achieves L2=0.593 (−6.8% vs. baseline). The augmentation is orthogonal to the planning head architecture: it improves robustness to scene orientation, which should complement the endpoint attention mechanism.

**Auxiliary 2D supervision** (`aux2d` batch, 2026-04-15). Training with an auxiliary image-space detection head at FPN level 0 substantially improves stage-1 feature quality. The resulting pretrain (`ptaux2d`) combined with `planpredtrajdeformmm` achieves L2=0.560–0.562, CR=0.037–0.062% — a 12% L2 reduction and 53–72% collision reduction over baseline. The aux-2D benefit flows entirely through improved camera feature quality: the stage-2 architecture is unchanged.

**Auxiliary 2.5D supervision** (`aux2p5d`, 2026-04-15/23). Extending `SparseDriveAux2DHead` with a per-object log-depth regression branch (`SparseDriveAux2p5DHead`) adds a small but consistent improvement over plain aux2d at stage 1: det mAP 0.441 vs. 0.437 (+0.004), NDS 0.555 vs. 0.546 (+0.009), map mAP 0.571 vs. 0.570 (+0.001). The depth branch is supervised by L1 on log(d) at Hungarian-matched positives; the additional signal provides a complementary 3D geometric constraint without dominating the existing aux losses. The resulting `sparsedrive_stage1_aux2p5d.pth` pretrain was evaluated at stage 2 via `ptaux2p5d_ppdeformmm_planifls` (Narval job 59814262, DGX job 3636); results are discussed in the Results and Discussion sections below.

Because the three improvements operate at distinct levels — the stage-2 augmentation pipeline, the stage-2 planning decoder architecture, and the stage-1 backbone training objective — their effects are expected to be largely additive. No component modifies the same pathway as another: rot3dv2 operates on the data augmentation before any model forward pass; the planning head changes operate on the decoder query and attention mechanism; aux2d operates on the stage-1 FPN feature representation.

This report documents a systematic combination of all three, starting with the two most mature components (planning refinement and rotation augmentation) and progressively adding aux2d pretrain and, if validated, map DN.

## Method

All three configs are derived from `planpredtrajdeformmm_planinstfeat_laststage` (the best model from the `plan_refine` batch) with two orthogonal modifications applied independently and jointly.

**`ppdeformmm_planifls_rot3dv2`** adds rot3dv2 stage-2 augmentation (`rot3d_range=[-0.3925, 0.3925]`, ±22.5°) to the laststage planning head. Pretrain: public `sparsedrive_stage1.pth`.

**`ptaux2d_ppdeformmm_planifls`** upgrades the pretrain to `sparsedrive_stage1_aux2d.pth` (from the aux2d stage-1 run), replacing the public checkpoint. No augmentation change.

**`ptaux2d_ppdeformmm_planifls_rot3dv2`** applies both changes simultaneously: aux2d pretrain + rot3dv2 augmentation.

**`ptaux2p5d_ppdeformmm_planifls`** upgrades the pretrain further to `sparsedrive_stage1_aux2p5d.pth`, which was trained with the additional per-object log-depth regression branch. The stage-2 architecture and augmentation are identical to `ptaux2d_ppdeformmm_planifls`. This isolates the effect of the depth branch by varying only the pretrained weights.

**`ptaux2d_ppdeformmm_planifls_numdemapx2`** keeps the same aux2d pretrain and planning-head recipe, but doubles the planning context selection inside `MotionPlanningHead` from `num_det=50, num_map=10` to `num_det=100, num_map=20`. This tests whether the current planner is bottlenecked by how many detection and map instances it can condition on.

**`ptauxdnrot_ppdeformmm`** loads the new combined stage-1 pretrain `sparsedrive_stage1_dn_rot3d_aux2p5d.pth` (from the Apollo `stage1_8gpu_noflash_dn_rot3d_aux2p5d` run, which stacks det denoising + rot3d + aux2p5d at stage 1) on top of `planpredtrajdeformmm` without `planifls` and without stage-2 rot3d augmentation. This isolates the effect of the combined stage-1 stack against the matched-architecture `ppdeform` baseline.

**`ptauxdnrot_ppdeformmm_planifls`** stacks the same combined pretrain on the `ppdeformmm_planifls` recipe to test whether the planning-head refinement recovers planning gains on top of the stronger stage-1 features. Killarney `3305029` (completed).

**`ptaux2p5d_ppdeformmm`** loads the aux2p5d-only stage-1 pretrain (`sparsedrive_stage1_aux2p5d.pth`) on top of `planpredtrajdeformmm` without `planifls`. Pairs with the existing `ptaux2p5d_ppdeformmm_planifls` runs (Narval 59814262, DGX 3636) to give an aux2p5d-pretrain `planifls`-vs-no-`planifls` delta on the same host, mirroring the `ptauxdnrot` pair. Killarney `3310795` (queued 2026-04-28).

That tradeoff is small in absolute terms, but it reverses the hoped-for planning gain from stacking all three components.

## Discussion

The strongest conclusion from the combined batch is that aux2d pretraining transfers cleanly into the refined planning head, but rot3dv2 does not add planning gains on top of that stronger stage-1 initialization. The aux2d-only combined model improves the key planning metrics over `ppdeformmm_planifls_rot3dv2`: lower `L2` on both hosts, better `obj_box_col` on DGX, lower `car_ade` on both hosts, and higher `car_epa`/`ped_epa` on both hosts. This is consistent with the earlier hypothesis that better camera features are directly useful to the endpoint-attention planning decoder.

Compared against the historical baselines, the current picture is sharper than before. `ppdeform` to `ppdeform_planifls` was a clean planning win in the April 2 batch. `ppdeform` to `ptaux2d + ppdeform` was also a clean win in the April 15 aux2d batch. But the three-way stack is not monotone: `ptaux2d + ppdeform_planifls` wins on L2, while `ptaux2d + ppdeform_planifls + rot3dv2` wins on detection, and neither cleanly dominates the best earlier collision numbers.

The full combination is still interesting because its perception quality is the best in the report. `NDS`, `mAP`, and `ped_ade` all improve versus aux2d-only, suggesting that rot3dv2 remains valuable for representation robustness and scene understanding. However, that detection-side gain does not translate into lower planning L2 or collision. The likely interpretation is not that rot3dv2 is harmful in general, but that the current planning decoder or training balance does not convert those perception improvements into better ego trajectory selection.

For planning-first model selection, `ptaux2d_ppdeformmm_planifls` is the current winner and should replace the rot3dv2 variants as the mainline checkpoint. For detection-first or balanced multi-task selection, `ptaux2d_ppdeformmm_planifls_rot3dv2` remains competitive because it sets competitive `NDS` and `mAP` while staying well ahead of the SparseDrive planning baseline; `ptaux2p5d_ppdeformmm_planifls` pushes detection further (`NDS=0.5550`, `mAP=0.4461` on DGX) but at a planning cost on that host.

The `ptaux2p5d` result extends the pattern seen with rot3dv2: improvements to the representation quality of the stage-1 backbone help detection reliably, but do not transfer cleanly to planning. Unlike rot3dv2 (which consistently hurt planning on both hosts), the depth branch is more ambiguous — it marginally helps on Narval and clearly hurts on DGX. This cross-host variance suggests the depth branch may be operating near the noise floor of the planning evaluation, and that the signal-to-noise ratio on planning is too low to call it a reliable gain. Running a DGX repeat of the Narval-favourable result would be needed to resolve the inconsistency, but given the small Narval margin, the null hypothesis (depth branch is planning-neutral) cannot be ruled out.

`ptauxdnrot_ppdeformmm` reinforces the same pattern at greater scale. Stacking det DN + rot3d + aux2p5d at stage 1 produces the strongest perception in the family but does not convert into planning gains under the matched `ppdeform` architecture; in fact it underperforms the `ppdeform` baseline on both `L2` and `obj_box_col`. The natural reading is that stronger stage-1 features alone are not sufficient — planning gains in this codebase have, so far, only materialized when paired with the planning-head refinement (`planifls`). The `planifls` follow-up `ptauxdnrot_ppdeformmm_planifls` (Killarney 3305029) confirms that pairing helps but does not flip the conclusion: `planifls` recovers ~0.04 of `L2` (`0.5612 → 0.5216`) on top of the richer pretrain, yet still finishes `+0.0159` behind the aux2d-only `ptaux2d_ppdeformmm_planifls` leader on `L2` and `+0.047` pp behind on `obj_box_col`. So the dn+rot3d+aux2p5d pretrain transfers losslessly to detection (matching the no-`planifls` mAP_normal nearly 1:1) but yields no planning gain over aux2d-only even with the strongest planning-head recipe in this report — the planning ceiling in this family is set by the planner, not by the pretrain.

The `numdemapx2` null result is informative: at `num_det=50, num_map=10`, the `MotionPlanningHead` is not capacity-limited by the number of instances available for planning. If context size is not the bottleneck, the limitation is more likely in the quality of the per-instance features or the attention mechanism itself.

## Future Work

1. Recenter the next planning experiments on `ptaux2d_ppdeformmm_planifls`, since it is the best and most reproducible planning checkpoint in the combined family.
2. Reevaluate `planwp` from the aux2d-only starting point. The earlier planwp gain was measured in the laststage batch, but the new best base model has changed.

5. The `ptaux2p5d` planning result is cross-host inconsistent. If aux2p5d is revisited for planning, run a DGX repeat of the Narval result before drawing conclusions; alternatively, tune the depth regression loss weight to reduce the detection-vs-planning tradeoff.
